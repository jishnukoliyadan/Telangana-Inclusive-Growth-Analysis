## Summary and Conclusion to Problem Statements

Telangana’s erratic rainfall forces farmers to buy mismatched equipment (e.g., water-intensive tractors in dry zones), wasting subsidies and inflating costs. Broken infrastructure worsens floods/droughts, while slow e-Stamp fund transfers and undervalued land records hurt rural businesses.

1. Agriculture: Floods/droughts idle machinery; subsidies fund tools incompatible with local conditions (e.g., paddy tractors in arid zones).
2. MSME Delays: Small firms face long approvals (pending ₹3,200cr incentives) vs. fast-tracked mega projects, pushing entrepreneurs into debt.
3. Digital Gaps: e-Stamps boost revenue but exclude rural areas; land digitization lags, limiting farmers’ market access.
4. Inclusion Barriers : SC/ST job quotas aim to uplift marginalized groups, but approval delays and urban job clustering harm rural MSME growth.

These issues were identified through news analysis, including gaps in addressing marginalized groups, as the sub-classification Gazette notification was not published during the initial project proposal submission. This indicates the initial intuitions on government might have more focus on marginalized communities were proved to be correct.
